# Native LLM Techniques for Ranking & Recommendation: A Critical Research Review (2024–mid-2026)

## TL;DR
- **The LLM paradigm is genuinely transferring to recommendation** — power-law scaling now demonstrated in production at Meta (HSTU, +12.4% online), Kuaishou (OneRec), Netflix, YouTube (PLUM), and LinkedIn (360Brew) — but the wins come from *architecture, scale, and sequence modeling native to recommendation data*, not from bolting a text-LLM onto a ranker. Off-the-shelf open LLMs (Qwen3, Llama, Mistral, Gemma) dominate as *rerankers in IR/RAG*, but as *core recommenders* they still frequently lose to well-tuned SASRec/HSTU-style models on production-realistic evaluations.
- **On the user's central question:** a versatile model (ranking + explanation + user embedding) makes ranking stronger *only conditionally*. Auxiliary tasks that are *correlated and discriminative* (genre/rating prediction, rationale distillation) act as regularizers and can lift ranking metrics; but forcing a discriminative ranking objective and an open-ended natural-language generation objective to share parameters produces a documented "seesaw"/negative-transfer effect that *hurts* ranking unless tasks are architecturally decoupled (MoE/LoRA experts, gradient detachment).
- **Practical verdict:** the winning industrial pattern in 2025–26 is a *dedicated generative recommender / foundation model* (HSTU/OneRec/PLUM/Netflix-FM/GEM style) for retrieval+ranking, optionally producing reusable user embeddings, with explanations treated as a *decoupled or downstream* task — not a monolithic single-tower that ranks and writes prose from one shared parameter set. Reproducibility caveats from the Dacrema/Rendle lineage mean you should trust *online A/B and production deployments* far more than offline NDCG/HR leaderboard deltas.

## Key Findings

**1. LLM techniques applied natively to recommenders — what's real:**
- **Meta HSTU ("Actions Speak Louder than Words," ICML 2024, arXiv 2402.17152):** reformulates ranking+retrieval as sequential transduction; up to 65.8% NDCG gains on public data, 5.3–15.2× faster than FlashAttention2 on 8192-length sequences, +12.4% online A/B, deployed at 1.5T params on billions of users. First production demonstration that **LLM-style power-law scaling holds for recommendation**.
- **Kuaishou OneRec / OneRec-V2 (arXiv 2506.13695, 2508.20900):** end-to-end generative model replacing retrieve→rank cascade. OneRec-V1: +0.54%/+1.24% App Stay Time online, 25% of QPS, opex only ~10.6% of traditional pipeline. OneRec-V2: lazy decoder-only, scaled to 8B params, 94% compute reduction, +0.467%/0.741% App Stay Time, 400M DAU. Uses RQ-Kmeans semantic IDs + DPO preference alignment. OneRec-Foundation (Jan 2026) built on Qwen3 backbone (1.7B & 8B).
- **Google TIGER (arXiv 2305.05065):** RQ-VAE semantic IDs + autoregressive decoding. On Amazon Beauty it reports up to **29% NDCG@5 improvement over SASRec and 17.3% Recall@5 over S3-Rec**; on Toys and Games, 21%/15% NDCG@5/NDCG@10 gains — i.e., gains are benchmark-specific and measured against different prior methods, not a single uniform "SOTA." **PLUM (YouTube/DeepMind, arXiv 2510.07784, WWW 2026):** adapts pretrained LLMs via Semantic IDs (SID-v2) + continued pretraining + generative retrieval fine-tuning; in live A/B vs the LEM+ production baseline it reports **Panel CTR +4.96% on Shorts and +0.76% on long-form video (LFV)**, plus Engaged Users +0.07%/+0.28% and Views +0.80%/+0.39% — beating a heavily-optimized embedding-table production model.
- **Netflix Foundation Model (Netflix Tech Blog, March 2025):** unified autoregressive transformer over interaction sequences replacing many specialized models; scaling laws confirmed from millions→billions of params; serves as predictor, embedding generator, or fine-tuning base.
- **Meta GEM (Engineering at Meta, Nov 10 2025):** LLM-scale ads foundation model reporting a **5% increase in ad conversions on Instagram and a 3% increase in ad conversions on Facebook Feed in Q2**, with Q3 architecture changes that "doubled the performance benefit per unit of data and compute." Meta describes GEM as **"4x more efficient at driving ad performance gains for a given amount of data and compute compared to Meta's original ads recommendation ranking models,"** referencing Wukong scaling-law work. Follow-ups: LLaTTE (arXiv 2601.20083), GPR (arXiv 2511.10138).
- **LinkedIn 360Brew (arXiv 2501.16450):** 150B decoder-only (Mixtral 8x22 MoE base), 30+ tasks, matches/exceeds task-specific production models on offline metrics without task-specific fine-tuning. Note: the arXiv version was withdrawn over a licensing issue.

**2. Off-the-shelf open models as rankers:**
- **Qwen3 Embedding/Reranker (arXiv 2506.05176):** Qwen3-Embedding-8B ranked **No.1 on the MTEB Multilingual leaderboard (score 70.58) as of June 5, 2025**, beating Gemini-Embedding, and scored 80.68 on MTEB-Code; Qwen3-Reranker-8B improves ranking ~3.0 pts over the 0.6B model. (Later leaderboard snapshots have been topped by NVIDIA's Llama-Embed-Nemotron-8B and BAAI's BGE-en-ICL at ~71.24 — so "SOTA" here is a moving target.) Qwen remains the strongest open ranking/embedding backbone family; Qwen3-VL-Embedding/Reranker (arXiv 2601.04720, Jan 2026) tops MMEB-V2 (77.8).
- **RankZephyr (arXiv 2312.02724), RankLLaMA, RankVicuna:** open listwise rerankers matching/surpassing GPT-4 on TREC DL + BEIR.
- **NVIDIA Nemotron rerankers:** llama-nemotron-rerank-1b-v2, nv-rerankqa-mistral-4b-v3; strong BEIR+multilingual; 1B pipeline 3.5× smaller than the 4B Mistral variant.
- **Reasoning rerankers:** Rank1 (arXiv 2502.18418, 635K R1 traces), Rank-R1 (GRPO RL), ReasonRank (arXiv 2508.07050). ReasonRank is 2–2.7× *faster* than pointwise Rank1 (one reasoning chain over a list vs per-passage chains).

**3. Critical assessment / where hype exceeds evidence:**
- **Reproducibility crisis (Dacrema et al. 2019 RecSys best paper; Rendle et al. 2020, 2022):** 11 of 12 reproducible neural recommenders were beaten by well-tuned simple baselines; "phantom progress" from weak baselines persists in 2024–25 studies (diffusion recs arXiv 2505.09364; intent-aware arXiv 2501.10143; SIGIR message-passing arXiv 2503.07823).
- **Scaling-law fragility:** HSTU scaling shown on MovieLens-20M but **stagnates on Amazon Beauty past ~8 layers** (UNGER, arXiv 2502.06269); "Scaling New Frontiers" (arXiv 2412.00714) finds HSTU beats SASRec/MBSTR but with dataset-dependent exceptions.
- LLM-as-recommender results are dataset-dependent: RecBench (arXiv 2503.05493) reports LLM recommenders beat conventional by up to +5% AUC / +170% NDCG@10, but this uses text-heavy setups where SASRec is the main conventional baseline; production-realistic temporal splits narrow or reverse the gap.

**4. Versatility (ranking + explanation + embeddings) — direct evidence:**
- **Helps (correlated/discriminative auxiliaries):** RDRec (ACL 2024, arXiv 2405.10587) rationale distillation lifts sequential rec +0.5–9.8% and top-N +12.1–42.2% H@k/N@k. "Architecture is All You Need" (arXiv 2506.15833) multi-task variant beat single-task on every metric (NDCG@10 0.0991→0.1357 on ML-10M).
- **Hurts (heterogeneous full sharing):** Rec-MoELoRA (ScienceDirect S2667295226000231, 2026) documents P5 full-parameter joint training of discriminative ranking + generative explanation falling into a "seesaw effect"; decoupling via MoE/LoRA recovers +4.3% HR@10 on Sports.
- **Netflix FM:** genre/multi-token auxiliary objectives described as regularizers that reduce overfitting and improve item-ID prediction (qualitative, no published deltas).

## Details

### Topic 1: Native application of LLM techniques to ranking/recommendation

The defining 2024–26 shift is treating recommendation as **sequential transduction / autoregressive generation over item tokens**, rather than as feature-engineered pointwise CTR prediction. Meta's HSTU established the template: heterogeneous features unified into a single interaction sequence, a custom attention variant (pointwise gating + element-wise U(X) projection) designed for high-cardinality, non-stationary vocabularies, and M-FALCON amortizing inference to serve 285× more complex models within the same compute budget. Its central scientific claim — that recommendation quality scales as a power law of training compute across three orders of magnitude — is the "ChatGPT moment" framing that motivated the whole wave.

The transferred techniques now well-documented in production are: (1) **tokenization via Semantic IDs** (RQ-VAE in TIGER; RQ-Kmeans in OneRec; SID-v2 with co-occurrence contrastive alignment in PLUM); (2) **autoregressive generation** of item IDs replacing dot-product ANN retrieval (TIGER, OneRec, PLUM); (3) **pretrain→fine-tune** paradigms (PLUM's continued pretraining on domain data; GEM's pretrain-then-transfer to the ads fleet); (4) **preference optimization / RLHF analogues** (OneRec's DPO / P-Score reward, OneRec-V2's Duration-Aware Reward Shaping + Gradient-Bounded Policy Optimization); (5) **long-context user-history modeling** (Netflix sparse/low-rank attention + sliding-window sampling; HSTU context parallelism, arXiv 2508.04711, RecSys 2025); and (6) **distillation** (YouTube's distillation+quantization noted at the Netflix PRS 2025 workshop).

**Does the scaling paradigm truly transfer?** Partially and conditionally. Production evidence (HSTU, OneRec, PLUM, Netflix, GEM) is strongly positive at industrial data scale. But public-dataset evidence is fragile: HSTU's power law holds on MovieLens-20M yet plateaus on Amazon Beauty past ~8 layers (UNGER), and "Towards Generalizable and Efficient Large-Scale Generative Recommenders" (arXiv 2605.23312) argues scaling headroom is *task-dependent* — long-horizon taste tasks benefit, but short-horizon engagement and availability-driven tasks may saturate, where "objective or serving changes may matter more" than capacity. The honest reading: scale fixes *capacity*, not *objective mismatch, freshness, popularity bias, or exploration* (per the "Why Generative Recommendation Still Cannot Replace the Full Ranking Pipeline" analysis, June 2026).

### Topic 2: Off-the-shelf open models as rankers

For **information-retrieval reranking** (as opposed to core recommendation), fine-tuned open LLMs are the clear state of the art. The **Qwen family is the strongest backbone**: Qwen3-Embedding-8B topped MTEB Multilingual (70.58) and MTEB-Code (80.68), beating Gemini-Embedding at that snapshot; the Qwen3 reranker series (0.6B/4B/8B) with instruction-awareness and 32K context leads text reranking (Qwen3-Reranker-4B MTEB-R 69.76; 8B CMTEB-R 77.45, MTEB-Code 81.22). The Qwen3-VL-Embedding/Reranker extension (arXiv 2601.04720, Jan 2026) tops MMEB-V2 (77.8). NVIDIA's Nemotron/NeMo Retriever line (llama-nemotron-rerank-1b/vl, nv-rerankqa-mistral-4b-v3, llama-embed-nemotron-8b — SOTA multilingual MTEB Oct 2025) targets commercially-licensed, latency-optimized RAG. E5-Mistral and Gemma-based rerankers (RankT5, Gemma reranker) round out the field.

**Backbone choice matters, but objective and data matter more.** "Reproducing and Comparing Distillation Techniques for Cross-Encoders" (arXiv 2603.03010) finds the *training objective* (InfoNCE/MarginMSE ≫ pointwise BCE) rivals or exceeds the benefit of a larger backbone — practically decisive for latency-constrained serving. Rank-DistiLLM (arXiv 2405.07920) shows cross-encoders distilled from LLM teachers, *with* hard-negative sampling + deep sampling + listwise loss, **reach LLM effectiveness while being orders of magnitude more efficient** — the strongest evidence for the "distill the 70B into a small cross-encoder" playbook.

**When does a small fine-tuned cross-encoder beat a prompted 70B?** When latency/cost is bounded and the domain is in-distribution: cross-encoders precompute nothing but score fast, produce calibratable numeric scores, and (per practitioner reports) an embedding alone sometimes matches a weak-reranker stack. LLM rerankers win on reasoning-intensive, out-of-distribution, or negation-heavy queries where relevance requires inference. **Contamination caveat:** RankZephyr's own robustness argument (NovelEval, post-training-cutoff queries) exists precisely because benchmark overfitting/contamination is a known confounder; embedding models like GTE/Arctic are trained on BEIR tasks (FEVER, SCIDOCS), inflating their apparent zero-shot strength (arXiv 2505.19274).

### Topic 3: The versatility question — ranking + explanation + user embeddings

This is the user's crux, so the evidence is worth stating precisely.

**(a) Auxiliary tasks as regularization / knowledge injection — HELPS ranking.**
- **RDRec (ACL 2024):** adds explanation + rationale-generation (distilled via Llama-2-7B CoT) on the POD backbone; sequential rec improves 0.5–9.8% and top-N 12.1–42.2% on Amazon Sports/Beauty/Toys (e.g., Beauty top-N H@1 0.0846→0.1203, +42.2%, p<1e-14). Ablation confirms the rationale tasks drive the gains. **Caveat:** review-data leakage may inflate magnitudes (author-acknowledged), and the model sometimes "learned to prioritize predicting user-item interaction over considering the rationale" — i.e., the explanation is not always faithful.
- **"Architecture is All You Need" (arXiv 2506.15833, Comcast, June 2025):** multi-task LSRec (genre rec, rating prediction, movie-by-genre/rating auxiliaries) beat single-task on *every* metric and model size on ML-10M (LSRec-small NDCG@10 0.0991→0.1357; HR@1 0.0360→0.0584). Note these auxiliaries are *discriminative*, not NL generation.
- **Netflix FM:** genre-derived auxiliary targets + multi-token prediction described as regularizers reducing overfitting on noisy item-ID prediction and improving hierarchical prediction accuracy (qualitative; no numeric deltas published).

**(b) Neutral / conditional.** Google's USER-LLM (arXiv 2402.13598) and related user-embedding work show LLM-derived user embeddings (from a dedicated user encoder integrated via cross-attention) outperform text-prompt personalization on long-history tasks with up to 78.1× compute reduction — but this is *embedding quality* transferring, not proof that a single ranking model's embeddings are better than a dedicated embedding model's. Recent work (TokenMinds arXiv 2606.25147; "How Do Decoder-Only LLMs Perceive Users" arXiv 2602.10622) pursues pretrained user tokens/embeddings but does not establish that generation-model embeddings beat purpose-built ones for ranking.

**(c) Task interference — HURTS ranking.** The MTL recommendation literature robustly documents **negative transfer and the seesaw phenomenon** from gradient conflict on shared parameters (PLE/MMoE lineage; survey arXiv 2502.21195; MultiBalance arXiv 2411.11871; STEM arXiv 2308.13537). The most direct evidence for the *ranking + explanation* case: **Rec-MoELoRA (2026)** shows P5 full-parameter joint training of discriminative sequential ranking and open-ended explanation generation "falls into an obvious seesaw effect"; the gradients "point in conflicting or even opposite directions." Decoupling into MoE/LoRA experts recovers +4.3% HR@10 (and +3.7% BLEU-4) on Sports. OneRank (arXiv 2606.16838) similarly needs three-level decoupling (input task tokens, information-flow decoupling, prediction-level gradient detachment) to get forward knowledge-sharing without backward interference. Fundamentally, "Can Optimization Trajectories Explain Multi-Task Transfer?" (arXiv 2408.14677) warns that *which* tasks help vs. hurt is **not reliably predictable a priori** from gradient conflict alone.

**Chain-of-thought / reasoning for ranking:** Rank1, Rank-R1, ReasonRank show reasoning helps IR reranking on reasoning-intensive benchmarks (BRIGHT, R2MED). But for *recommendation* ranking specifically, the evidence is thinner and more mixed — 360Brew frames reasoning as an architectural advantage (bigger model + longer context → better ranking) rather than presenting a CoT-on/off ablation with numbers. Treat "reasoning improves recommendation ranking" as promising but not yet cleanly demonstrated with production ranking deltas.

### Topic 4: Synthesis — winning architecture patterns 2025–26

Three patterns coexist:
1. **Dedicated generative recommenders (HSTU/OneRec/PLUM/Netflix-FM/GEM):** winning at the largest industrial scale for retrieval+ranking. Trained from recommendation data, using LLM *techniques* (autoregression, scaling, semantic IDs, RLHF-analogues) but *not* text-LLM backbones (except PLUM/OneRec-Foundation which adapt pretrained LLMs). Highest ranking quality + best scaling, highest training cost, serving solved via M-FALCON/lazy-decoder/quantization.
2. **Adapted open LLMs as rankers/rerankers (Qwen3, Llama, Nemotron):** winning in IR/search/RAG reranking; usable in recommendation via semantic-ID adaptation (PLUM, OneRec-Foundation) but latency/cost forces distillation to small cross-encoders for production.
3. **Hybrid two-tower + LLM reranker:** pragmatic; cheap ANN retrieval → cross-encoder/LLM reranker on top-k. Best when you cannot retrain a foundation model but want a relevance boost.

**Trade-off summary:** dedicated GRs = best ranking + embeddings, highest train cost; open-LLM rerankers = best explainability + fastest iteration, worst latency; distilled cross-encoders = best latency/cost, need good teachers + hard negatives.

## Recommendations

**Staged plan for a production ranking system:**

1. **Start with a strong, honestly-tuned baseline.** Given the Dacrema/Rendle reproducibility findings, benchmark any generative/LLM recommender against a *well-tuned* SASRec/HSTU and matrix-factorization baseline under *temporal* (not leave-one-out) splits before believing offline gains. **Threshold to proceed:** a generative model must show consistent lift on production-realistic temporal splits AND a positive online A/B, not just offline NDCG.

2. **For core ranking, invest in a dedicated generative recommender (HSTU/OneRec/PLUM-style), not a text-LLM monolith.** Adopt Semantic IDs (RQ-VAE/RQ-Kmeans with collaborative-signal alignment) and autoregressive generation. Use continued pretraining if adapting an open LLM (PLUM blueprint). **Benchmark that changes this:** if your item catalog is small/stable and history short, a tuned two-tower + cross-encoder may match a GR at far lower cost.

3. **On versatility: keep ranking primary; add auxiliaries selectively and decoupled.** Add *discriminative, correlated* auxiliaries (genre/category/rating prediction, multi-token prediction) — these regularize and reliably help (Netflix, LSRec). Add *explanation generation* only via **decoupled parameters** (MoE/LoRA experts, gradient detachment) — never full parameter sharing, which triggers the seesaw effect (Rec-MoELoRA, OneRank). **Threshold:** if adding explanation drops offline ranking metrics >~1–2% with shared params, isolate the task or move it downstream.

4. **Generate user embeddings from the foundation model as a reusable byproduct** (Netflix/USER-LLM pattern) — this is well-supported and high-leverage across downstream tasks. But validate they beat a dedicated embedding model on *your* ranking task before decommissioning the latter.

5. **For reranking / search surfaces, use fine-tuned open backbones (Qwen3 first choice) and then distill** into a small cross-encoder with InfoNCE/MarginMSE + hard negatives + listwise loss (Rank-DistiLLM). Prompted 70B rerankers only for reasoning-intensive, low-QPS, high-value flows.

6. **Guard against contamination and hype:** prefer benchmarks/queries after model training cutoff (NovelEval-style), report online metrics, and be skeptical of any single-dataset scaling-law claim.

## Caveats
- **Peer-review status:** HSTU (ICML 2024), PLUM (WWW 2026), TIGER (NeurIPS 2023), RDRec (ACL 2024), context-parallelism HSTU (RecSys 2025) are venue-published. OneRec/OneRec-V2, GEM, LLaTTE, GPR, 360Brew, and the Netflix FM blog are **preprints or engineering blogs, not peer-reviewed**; 360Brew's arXiv paper was *withdrawn* over licensing. Industry A/B numbers are self-reported and unaudited.
- **Offline vs. production:** most academic LLM-recommender wins are offline-only; the Dacrema/Rendle lineage shows these frequently fail to survive strong baselines and proper evaluation. Only HSTU/OneRec/PLUM/GEM/Netflix report production deployment at scale.
- **The versatility answer is conditional, not universal.** "Does one versatile model make ranking stronger?" → Yes if auxiliaries are correlated and architecturally decoupled; No (seesaw) if a discriminative ranker and open-ended generator fully share parameters. Multi-task transfer outcomes are not reliably predictable a priori (arXiv 2408.14677).
- **Faithfulness of explanations:** even when explanation auxiliaries help ranking, the generated rationales can be unfaithful (RDRec) — do not treat LLM ranking explanations as causal accounts of the model's decision.
- **Metric interpretation:** benchmark leaderboards move fast (Qwen3's June 2025 MTEB #1 has since been surpassed by NVIDIA Llama-Embed-Nemotron-8B and BGE-en-ICL), and industrial lifts are relative to each company's own prior baseline — cross-company comparisons of "+X%" are not apples-to-apples.
- **Recency:** figures current as of mid-2026; several cited works (OneRec-Foundation, Qwen3-VL, LLaTTE, TokenMinds) are dated Jan–June 2026 and rapidly evolving.
