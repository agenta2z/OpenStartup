# LLM-Native Ranking for Recommendation Systems: A Critical Survey of SOTA and the Multimodal Question

## TL;DR
- "LLM-native ranking" is not one thing but a convergence of your two interpretations: **interpretation (1)** — autoregressive sequence modeling over behavioral/ID tokens (HSTU, PinFM, Netflix FM) — supplies the *training paradigm and scaling laws*, while **interpretation (2)** — semantic/multimodal tokenization (TIGER, OneRec, QARM) — supplies the *vocabulary*. The frontier system is "multimodal-grounded semantic IDs consumed by an autoregressive/generative backbone," which is exactly the unification you suspected.
- **Multimodal is the natural direction for the item-representation layer, but not a wholesale replacement for behavioral modeling.** The strongest, most reproducible evidence for multimodal is concentrated in cold-start/long-tail and content-rich domains (short video, e-commerce, visual discovery); in dense head-item regimes, behavioral/collaborative ID signals still dominate, and content-only or naive multimodal features historically underperform or even hurt mature rankers unless explicitly aligned to the collaborative space.
- The single most important open problem is the **semantic–behavioral alignment / quantization-loss bottleneck**: distilling rich content embeddings into discrete semantic IDs discards information and caps scaling gains, which is why nearly every serious industrial system (Kuaishou RQ-Kmeans/QARM, Xiaohongshu IDProxy/DAS) now injects collaborative signals into the tokenizer or aligns MLLM outputs to the ID embedding distribution rather than using raw content embeddings.

## Key Findings

1. **Two interpretations, one converging architecture.** HSTU/GR (Zhai et al., "Actions Speak Louder than Words," ICML 2024, arXiv 2402.17152) proved that pure sequential transduction over ID/action tokens obeys LLM-style power-law scaling and beats DLRMs at industrial scale — the paper reports "HSTU-based Generative Recommenders, with 1.5 trillion parameters, improve metrics in online A/B tests by 12.4% and have been deployed on multiple surfaces of a large internet platform with billions of users," alongside up to 65.8% NDCG on public data and a 5.3×–15.2× speedup over FlashAttention2 Transformers on 8192-length sequences. This is interpretation (1) — "LLM-inspired architecture, no semantics." The TIGER lineage (Rajput et al., NeurIPS 2023) proved items can be tokenized into content-derived semantic IDs and generated autoregressively. OneRec fuses both: HSTU-style scaling + RQ-Kmeans multimodal semantic IDs + RL alignment. SOTA is the fusion, not either pole.

2. **The cascade-replacement debate is real and unresolved.** OneRec (Kuaishou) claims to replace the entire retrieve→pre-rank→rank→re-rank cascade with one end-to-end generative model; Meta's GR and Pinterest's PinFM instead *upgrade the ranker/user-encoder in place* while keeping cascade structure. Both report wins, so this is an architectural choice, not a settled question.

3. **Multimodal helps most where behavioral signal is thin.** Consistent, repeated finding across Xiaohongshu IDProxy (2× cold-start AUC on new content), Kuaishou QARM (large ads revenue lift on cold items), and Netflix FM (metadata-based embeddings for unseen titles). But Hi-SAM and others show multimodal SIDs still do *not* uniformly beat sparse-ID methods on overall/warm ranking.

4. **The semantic-ID information bottleneck limits scaling.** Liu et al., "Understanding Generative Recommendation with Semantic IDs from a Model-scaling View" (arXiv 2509.25522, KDD 2026) find that "the performance of SID-based GR quickly saturates as we enlarge each component — the modality encoder, the quantization tokenizer, and the RS itself," while text-based GR (feeding item text directly to an LLM) "achieves up to 20% improvement over the best achievable performance of SID-based GR through scaling" — at much higher inference cost.

5. **Pure text-token LLM rankers remain impractical for high-QPS scoring.** Latency, calibration, position-invariance and cost issues push text LLMs into offline feature-generation / distillation / reranking-of-top-K roles, not into the fine-ranking hot path.

## Details

### A. Taxonomy of LLM-native ranking paradigms (2023–2026)

**(1) LLM-inspired sequence modeling over ID/behavioral tokens (no text semantics).**
- **Meta HSTU / Generative Recommenders (Zhai et al., ICML 2024, arXiv 2402.17152).** Reformulates ranking + retrieval as sequential transduction; HSTU modifies attention for high-cardinality non-stationary vocabularies. Deployed at 1.5T params with a reported +12.4% online metric improvement across surfaces of a billions-user platform; first demonstration that LLM scaling laws hold in production recsys. M-FALCON amortizes inference to serve 285× more complex models within the same budget. Notably, the paper explicitly *simplifies away* most features and uses no content/text semantics — it treats user actions as "an underexplored modality."
- **Meta ExFM (External Large Foundation Model, arXiv 2502.17494, WWW'25 Companion).** Serves trillion-parameter (up to 3.2T, >98% in embedding tables) ads models via external distillation into small servable "student" verticals; deployed at Meta >2 years. This is the distillation-based hybrid path, not generative.
- **Meta "Towards Generalizable and Efficient Large-Scale Generative Recommenders" (2026, arXiv 2605.23312)** and **"Bending the Scaling Law Curve" (arXiv 2602.16986).** Scale a GR from 2M→1B backbone params; key finding: scaling is "useful but uneven" — task-dependent, some tasks hit an empirical ceiling. Uses multi-token prediction for serving-latency alignment, sampled softmax + a projected decoding head for cheap retraining, and semantic item towers with collaborative-embedding masking for cold-start. This is Meta beginning to inject semantic/content signals into GR.
- **Netflix Foundation Model (Netflix Tech Blog, March 2025).** Autoregressive next-token transformer over tokenized interaction histories, replacing dozens of specialized models across 300M+ users; scales from millions→billions of params; confirms scaling laws hold; handles cold-start via metadata-based embeddings + attention weighting by item "age"; multi-token prediction for long-term intent. Draws from LLMs but keeps recsys-specific adaptations (interaction tokenization, request-time vs post-action features, KV caching).
- **Pinterest PinFM (RecSys 2025) and PinRec (arXiv 2504.10507).** PinFM: 20B-param transformer pretrained on 2 years of activity, integrated into downstream rankers, deployed to >half a billion users; deliberately uses categorical ID features (not pretrained item embeddings) for pretraining efficiency, with a deduplicated cross-attention transformer that caches user-sequence KVs and broadcasts to many candidates. PinRec: outcome-conditioned multi-token generative retrieval. UniPinRec (2026) unifies retrieval + ranking in one model/training stage.
- **ByteDance/Douyin: RankMixer (arXiv 2507.15551), LONGER, STCA.** RankMixer is an MLP-Mixer-style hardware-efficient ranker (not generative) deployed on Douyin Feed: +0.3% active days, +1% app duration; demonstrates a "steep scaling law." Complementary to HLLM (RankMixer fuses rich representations at the ranking stage; HLLM produces them).

**(2) Semantic ID approaches.**
- **Google TIGER (Rajput et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2305.05065).** RQ-VAE over Sentence-T5 content embeddings → hierarchical semantic IDs; seq2seq transformer autoregressively generates the next item's SID. Reports a 29% NDCG@5 improvement (vs SASRec) and 17.3% Recall@5 improvement (over S3-Rec) on Amazon Beauty, with ~20× memory reduction vs. atomic IDs; the hierarchical prefix structure enables cold-start generalization. Established the standard recipe: content encode → residual quantize → autoregressive SID prediction.
- **Google "Better Generalization with Semantic IDs" (Singh et al., RecSys 2024, arXiv 2306.08121).** Applied SIDs in a production YouTube CTR *ranking* model (not retrieval). Content embeddings = VideoBERT over audio+visual features → 2048-d topicality vectors; RQ-VAE with 8 levels × 2048 codebook; SentencePiece (SPM) tokenization of SIDs adapts them into the ranker. Key results (reported as % over a random-hashing baseline in figures): dense content embeddings *alone hurt* vs. random-hashed video IDs (memorization lost); Bigram-/SPM-SIDs beat random hashing especially on cold-start (CTR/1D AUC) when user history is present; SPM-SID generalizes best at large embedding tables. Bottom line: SIDs improve new/long-tail generalization "without sacrificing overall model quality." A 0.1% CTR AUC change is significant for their model. SIDs were also shown stable across RQ-VAE trained 6 months apart.
- **Google LIGER (arXiv 2411.18814).** Hybrid dense+generative: generative retrieval produces candidates, dense retrieval reranks/handles cold-start, mitigating pure-generative cold-start weakness.
- **Baidu COBRA (2025).** Cascaded sparse SID → dense vector with BeamFusion inference.
- **Snap GRID ("Generative Recommendation with Semantic IDs: A Practitioner's Handbook," CIKM 2025).** Open framework; standard practitioner reference.
- **RQ-Kmeans (Kuaishou), LETTER, ETEGRec, DAS, ADA-SID, LC-Rec** — all variants injecting collaborative/behavioral signal into the tokenizer to close the semantic-behavioral gap.

**(3) True text-token LLM rankers.**
- P5, M6-Rec, RecFormer, instruction-tuned rankers, RankGPT-style listwise rerankers. Industrial reality: pointwise LLM scoring is uncalibrated and ~10× costlier than specialized rerankers with lower accuracy; listwise gives modest NDCG gains (e.g., ~0.78 vs 0.74 NDCG@10) at ~9× cost and ~35× latency, and suffers position-dependence (same candidates, different order → different ranking, an architectural artifact of causal attention + positional encodings). Verdict: text LLMs viable only as top-K rerankers or teachers, not the fine-ranking hot path.
- **Alibaba RecGPT (arXiv 2507.22879) and RecGPT-V2 (arXiv 2512.14503).** LLM-driven, intent-centric pipeline (user-interest mining, item tag prediction, explanation generation) fully deployed on Taobao; RecGPT-V2 uses hierarchical multi-agent reasoning and reportedly cuts GPU usage 60%. This is LLM-for-recommendation as a reasoning/feature layer, not an LLM CTR scorer.
- **ByteDance HLLM (arXiv 2409.12740).** Hierarchical LLMs — item LLM + user LLM — for sequential recommendation; reports a +0.705% online ranking lift with no added inference latency (via caching); deployed in Douyin search advertising.

**(4) Hybrid architectures (LLM/MLLM → conventional ranker).** The dominant *deployed* industrial pattern for multimodal:
- **Kuaishou QARM (arXiv 2411.11739, CIKM 2025).** Diagnoses "Representation Unmatching" (MLLM trained on NLP/CV objectives ≠ recsys objective) and "Representation Unlearning" (cached, non-trainable features). Fixes: item-item alignment fine-tuning of the MLLM + quantitative codes turned into trainable code-IDs fed into downstream models for end-to-end MLLM information training.
- **Xiaohongshu IDProxy (Zhang et al., arXiv 2603.01590, under review 2026).** An InternVL MLLM generates "proxy" embeddings via an `[EMB]` token contrastively aligned to the *actual ID embedding distribution*, then injected into the ranker's atomic ID slots (structural reuse, not a side feature). Reports a "2x AUC improvement for new content" (posts published within 24h) and "+1.93% increase in Advertiser Value" vs global traffic, deployed in Content Feed (Aug 2025) and Display Ads (Mar 2025) A/B tests. Explicitly documents that naive multimodal embeddings and shallow MLP mappers give only marginal gains due to the semantic-collaborative distribution mismatch (industrial ID embeddings are irregular/non-clustered).
- **Xiaohongshu NoteLLM / NoteLLM-2 (WWW'24, KDD'25).** MLLM note representation (text+image) via end-to-end vision-encoder+LLM fusion (mICL + late fusion); deployed in Xiaohongshu's production recommendation system.
- **Meta "Quantizing Intent" (RQ-FSQ, arXiv 2606.01396).** Cross-domain semantic IDs from organic activity for ads CTR; RQ-FSQ discretizer matches dense-embedding AUC at ~280× storage reduction; reports +1.522% for users with near-zero ad interaction history — direct evidence of cross-domain behavioral transfer surviving the discrete bottleneck.

**(5) Multimodal generative recommenders (the frontier).**
- **Kuaishou OneRec / OneRec-V2 / OneMall.** OneRec (Technical Report, arXiv 2506.13695, Zhou et al.) is the flagship end-to-end generative system: encoder-decoder, collaborative-aware multimodal tokenizer integrating video titles/tags/audio/images with user behavior via RQ-Kmeans → hierarchical SIDs (miniCPM-V-8B embeddings + QFormer + hierarchical k-means → 3 codes/video); session-wise list generation; RL preference alignment (DPO / ECPO). Reported: "+0.54% and +1.24% in APP Stay Time, and +0.05% and +0.08% in LT7" on the Kuaishou main and Lite feeds (400M DAU), at "operating expense that is only 10.6% of traditional recommendation pipelines," handling 25% of total QPS, and +21.01% GMV growth in Local Life Service (100% QPS). Coverage was disclosed at ~25% of main-app/Lite traffic (Aug 2025 investor event), targeting 50–60%. OneRec-V2 (arXiv 2508.20900): a lazy decoder-only architecture (V1 spent 97.66% of compute on encoding) cut total computation 94% and training resources 90% and scaled to 8B params; online A/B (5% traffic, 1B-param model) gave +0.467% App Stay Time and +0.069% 7-day retention, at 36ms latency and 62% MFU on L20 GPUs, using GBPO RL on real user feedback. OneMall extends the family to e-commerce.
- **Kuaishou DAS (arXiv 2508.10584, CIKM'25).** One-stage dual-aligned semantic IDs jointly optimizing quantization + collaborative alignment (multi-view contrastive u2i/i2i/u2u), avoiding the two-stage information loss.
- **Hi-SAM (arXiv 2602.11799).** Reports multimodal SIDs (QARM, PSRQ+MCCA) beat HSTU by ~5% Cold GAUC on industrial data (0.5571 vs 0.5304) but do *not* uniformly beat sparse-ID methods on overall ranking — QARM is only +0.15% over HSTU on Book (0.6450 vs 0.6440) and *underperforms* HSTU on the Industrial dataset (0.6068 vs 0.6087) — a crucial honest datapoint.

### B. The multimodal question — critical verdict

**Evidence FOR multimodal as the natural direction:**
- Cold-start/long-tail is where every production system reports the biggest, most reproducible gains (IDProxy 2× cold AUC; QARM large cold-item ads lifts; Netflix metadata embeddings; Google SID CTR/1D AUC).
- Semantic IDs are increasingly *built from* multimodal encoders (OneRec: miniCPM-V; TIGER_M: CLIP/Qwen2-VL; UniSGR: Qwen3-VL), and content-rich domains (short video, e-commerce, visual discovery) are exactly where behavioral ID embeddings are information-poor for fresh items.
- Cross-domain transfer: content/semantic tokens transfer across domains where raw IDs cannot (Meta Quantizing Intent, universal-tokenization work).

**Evidence AGAINST / complicating:**
- The **ID-vs-modality debate** (Yuan et al., "Where to Go Next for Recommender Systems?", SIGIR 2023, arXiv 2303.13835): with a sequential backbone, MoRec can *outperform* IDRec for *text* and roughly match for *vision* — but this required strong modern encoders, and historically IDRec dominated by a large margin. In *warm/dense* regimes IDs remain very strong, and MoRec carried a large training-cost penalty (e.g., ~1 week on 8×A100 for one Swin-B configuration).
- Naive multimodal *hurts* mature rankers: Google found dense content embeddings alone underperform random-hashed IDs; Xiaohongshu found naive multimodal embeddings give only marginal gains without explicit alignment.
- Hi-SAM: multimodal SIDs have not uniformly surpassed sparse-ID methods on overall (warm) ranking.
- The **semantic-ID information bottleneck**: the Snap model-scaling paper (2509.25522) shows SID-based GR saturates and doesn't benefit from bigger encoders/tokenizers because quantization discards semantic information (an 11B encoder gives negligible gains over 77M; the ~3×256 tokenizer is the sweet spot, beyond which performance degrades).
- Head items are dominated by collaborative co-engagement; content similarity ≠ behavioral co-engagement (the "semantic gap").

**Synthesis.** SOTA is converging on **"multimodal-/collaboratively-grounded semantic tokenization + generative/LLM-style autoregressive sequence modeling."** Your interpretation (1) provides the scalable architecture and training objective; interpretation (2) provides a vocabulary that is (a) compact enough to serve and (b) rich enough to generalize to cold/long-tail items. The critical engineering insight from every serious deployment is that **raw multimodal content is not directly useful** — it must be *aligned to the collaborative/behavioral embedding space* (QARM item alignment, RQ-Kmeans collaborative SIDs, IDProxy ID-distribution alignment, DAS dual alignment) before it helps ranking. Multimodal is therefore the natural direction for the *item-representation and cold-start layer*, layered on top of — not replacing — behavioral sequence modeling.

### C. Technical deep-dive

**How multimodal signals get injected (ranking-specific tradeoffs):**
- **(a) Pre-fusion into semantic IDs (RQ-VAE / RQ-Kmeans / RQ-FSQ over VLM embeddings).** Compact, servable, cold-start-friendly, but lossy (quantization bottleneck caps scaling) and requires collaborative alignment to avoid semantic-gap failures. Best fit for retrieval + generative decoding; usable in ranking via n-gram/SPM hashing of SID subwords (Google).
- **(b) Side features / proxy embeddings in hybrid rankers.** Lowest disruption (reuse mature DLRM), preserves CTR calibration/multi-task heads; QARM/IDProxy show it works IF the MLLM output is aligned + trainable, not frozen/cached.
- **(c) Direct multimodal/text token sequences into an MLLM.** Highest ceiling and best scaling (Snap text-based GR, +20% over SID GR's ceiling), but inference cost is prohibitive for fine ranking of hundreds-thousands of candidates in tens of ms.
- **(d) Distillation.** Meta ExFM: large multimodal/foundation teacher → small servable student; decouples training scale from serving latency.

For ranking specifically, (b) and (d) preserve the multi-task CTR/CVR heads and calibration that generative next-token objectives do not natively provide — this is why OneRec still bolts on a reward-model/fine-ranking-like module and why pure generative decoding hasn't wholesale replaced pointwise scoring for calibrated CTR.

**Scaling laws — the contested core.** HSTU and OneRec claim clean power laws; Netflix confirms; but multiple papers push back: embedding-collapse work (arXiv 2310.04400) shows enlarging embeddings/interaction layers doesn't help DLRMs; the Snap paper shows SID-based GR saturates; Meta's own 2026 GR paper says scaling is "useful but uneven" with task-dependent ceilings. Verdict: scaling laws hold for the *backbone* of well-designed sequence models but are gated by item-representation capacity and objective/serving constraints.

**Action/target-aware attention.** HSTU interleaves item and action tokens. A 2026 critique ("Beyond Interleaving," arXiv 2603.10369) argues interleaving forces the Transformer to disentangle semantically incompatible signals, adding attention noise, and reformulates it as similarity-weighted action pooling — worth watching as a possible efficiency win for the GR ranker.

**Evaluation trust.** Offline: Recall@K/NDCG on Amazon/MovieLens (small, leave-one-out — fragile, per "How Reliable Are Semantic-ID Tokenizer Comparisons in Generative Recommendation?"). Online: app stay time, watch time, GMV, advertiser value. Treat single-vendor A/B percentages as directional; the most trustworthy claims (HSTU +12.4%, OneRec OPEX/stay-time, Google SID cold-start) are corroborated by deployment scale and multiple write-ups, but China-platform GMV/stay-time numbers come from technical reports/investor events, not peer review.

## Recommendations

1. **Adopt the unification explicitly.** Treat "LLM-native ranking" as an HSTU-style autoregressive backbone (which you already own) + a *semantic tokenization layer grounded in aligned multimodal content*. Don't frame (1) vs (2) as competing; they are the architecture and the vocabulary.
2. **Prioritize multimodal where your marginal behavioral signal is weakest:** cold-start, long-tail, fresh content, cross-surface transfer. Benchmark on *cold/1-day slices* (Google's CTR/1D AUC), not just aggregate CTR/NDCG — aggregate metrics will hide the win and can even regress (see Hi-SAM's warm-ranking result).
3. **Do not feed raw/frozen multimodal embeddings into the ranker.** Every deployment that worked (QARM, IDProxy, RQ-Kmeans, DAS) *aligned content to the collaborative/ID embedding space* and made it *trainable end-to-end under the CTR objective*. Budget for an alignment stage as a first-class component, not an afterthought.
4. **Stage the injection by latency budget:** start with (b) aligned proxy embeddings / trainable code-IDs as side features in the existing fine ranker (lowest risk, preserves calibration); pilot (a) multimodal semantic IDs for retrieval + cold-start; keep (c) full MLLM decoding for offline/reranking and (d) distillation for anything that must be served hot.
5. **Keep text-token LLMs off the fine-ranking hot path.** Use them as offline feature/profile generators, judges, data augmenters, and top-K rerankers (RecGPT/HLLM pattern), where their latency/calibration/position-invariance problems don't bite.
6. **Instrument the quantization-loss ceiling.** If you scale a SID-based GR and see early saturation, the bottleneck is likely SID information capacity (Snap finding), not backbone size — invest in richer/aligned tokenizers, add codebook levels only up to the ~3×256 sweet spot, or move to hybrid dense+SID (LIGER/COBRA) before buying more backbone params.

**Thresholds that change the plan:**
- If aligned multimodal SIDs stop beating sparse IDs even on cold slices → your content encoder or alignment is the problem, not the paradigm.
- If generative decoding can hit your candidate-scoring latency budget with calibrated multi-task heads → escalate from hybrid (b) toward end-to-end OneRec-style cascade replacement.
- If backbone scaling shows a clean power law with no saturation on your tasks → keep buying compute; if uneven/ceilinged (Meta 2026 finding) → shift investment to objectives and item representation.

## Caveats
- Much of the strongest multimodal/generative evidence is from **industry technical reports and arXiv preprints** (OneRec, QARM, IDProxy, ExFM, RankMixer), not peer-reviewed venues; A/B percentages are vendor-reported and not independently reproducible. Peer-reviewed anchors: HSTU (ICML'24), TIGER (NeurIPS'23), Google SID ranking (RecSys'24), Yuan et al. ID-vs-modality (SIGIR'23), NoteLLM-2 (KDD'25), the Snap model-scaling paper (KDD'26).
- Academic offline benchmarks (Amazon, MovieLens) are small and fragile; recent work questions the reliability of semantic-ID tokenizer comparisons on them.
- The cascade-replacement claim (OneRec) is a single-vendor result on short video; it is not established that one generative model beats a well-tuned cascade in e-commerce/ads at equal compute.
- "Scaling laws hold for recsys" is real but narrower than headlines: gated by embedding/representation capacity, task headroom, and serving/retraining constraints; contradicting evidence exists (embedding collapse, SID saturation, Meta's "uneven" finding).
- Several 2026 citations here are very recent preprints (Hi-SAM, IDProxy, Quantizing Intent, Beyond Interleaving, UniPinRec, OneMall); treat their specific numbers as provisional.
